\subsection*{Course Description}
02YMECH is a winter semester course for first-year bachelor students covering the basics of mechanics and some advanced methods, such as solving equations of motion for mechanical systems like a particle under constant force, a harmonic oscillator, the central force problem, and rigid bodies.

\vspace{2mm}
Each week, there will be \underline{four} 50-minute lectures and \underline{two} 50-minute exercise session. Lectures will cover theoretical concepts following the weekly schedule, while exercise sessions will focus on solving problems related to the lecture material. The instruction language is English.

\vspace{2mm}
02YMECHZ refers to the final exam for 02YMECH. The students who pass 02YMECH will be eligible to take 02YMECHZ. Please see the exam section for more details.

\subsection*{Prerequisite(s)} 
Knowledge of basic differentiation, integration, and trigonometry would be useful but are not necessary.

\subsection*{Credits: 4 (02YMECH) + 2 (02YMECHZ)}

\subsection*{\large Course Objectives} 
\begin{enumerate} \itemsep-0.4em
  \item To learn the basics of mechanics, and solve simple equations of motion.
  \item To understand the general framework for constructing and operating physical models.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Exams}

There will be \textbf{two midterm exams} and \textbf{one final exam} (02YMECHZ). Each exam will consist of \textbf{4 questions}, with each question worth \textbf{1 point}.

\begin{itemize}

	\item Students who score at least \textbf{4 points out of 8} across the \underline{two midterm exams} will be awarded the \textbf{Z (Zápočet) grade}. This means that they have passed 02YMECH, received 4 credits, and are eligible to take the final exam.

\item Students who are not awarded the Z grade but score \textbf{at least 1 point in total} across the two midterm exams may take a makeup exam upon request. The makeup exam will cover the same material as the 2nd Midterm Exam. To obtain the Z grade through the makeup exam, students must score \textbf{at least 5 points in total} across the two midterm exams and the makeup exam. Students who score less than 1 point across the two midterms, or who do not reach the required 5-point total after the makeup exam, must retake the course.

\item The final exam consists of a \textbf{written component} and an \textbf{oral component}.

\item Only students who score at least \textbf{2 points on the written part} may proceed to the oral examination. Students who score less than 2 points on the written part fail 02YMECHZ, and an \textbf{F grade} will be registered.

\item Attendance at the oral examination is \textbf{mandatory} for students who pass the written part.

\item The oral examination will be conducted as follows: Each student will randomly select a number from 1 to 16, corresponding to one of the sixteen topics listed at the end of this syllabus (see \emph{Topics for the Oral Part of the Final Exam}). Students will then have 30 minutes to prepare the chosen topic on blank paper, followed by a 5--10-minute oral examination based on that topic. Students are not required to provide all derivations unless otherwise stated, but they should be able to explain the main concepts and equations related to the selected topic.

\item The \textbf{final letter grade} is primarily determined by the written exam score. However, if a student demonstrates an outstanding performance during the oral examination, the final letter grade will be increased by one level. Conversely, if a student demonstrates a lack of understanding of the basic concepts of the course, the final letter grade will be decreased by one level. An average performance during the oral examination will not affect the final letter grade.

\item On request, the lecturer may allow a student to retake the final exam before the beginning of the next academic semester, \textbf{provided that the student has scored at least 1.5 points on the final exam}. Otherwise, the student must take the final exam in the next examination period.

\item Students who have already been awarded the Z grade but fail the final exam do not need to take the midterm exams again in the following academic year.


\end{itemize}

The exam dates will be announced in KOS during the second week of the semester. Students are responsible for registering for the exams in KOS. Students are required to bring their student ID cards to the exams.

\subsection*{Topics for the Oral Part of the Final Exam}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item How would you describe a circular motion in terms of a complex number? If you wanted the radius of the circular trajectory to decrease exponentially with time, how would you modify your description? What would be the connection of this description to damped oscillations?
	\item Derive work-energy theorem step-by-step starting from the definition of work and Newton's second law and explain its meaning.
	\item Define conservative force and potential energy by using the definition of work and explain why a conservative force can be expressed as the negative gradient of potential energy.
	\item Show mathematically step-by-step in detail why the total mechanical energy is conserved for a particle moving under a conservative force. 
	\item Use the conservation of mechanical energy as an equation of motion to derive the time dependence of position for a simple harmonic oscillator in one dimension.
	\item Derive step-by-step of total kinetic energy for discrete and continuous mass distributions starting from the definition of kinetic energy for a single particle and describe of how we define moment of inertia on the way.
	\item Describe projectile motion and derive expressions for time of flight, maximum height, range and trajectory equation.
	\item If the potential energy of a mechanical system can be described as an analytical function and if we are interested in small oscillations around the equilibrium point, show step-by-step how we can approximate the potential energy function using Taylor series expansion and derive the equation of motion in differential equation for for small oscillations. Do not solve it but memorize the general solution in terms of trigonometric functions.
	\item Starting from Hooke's law, derive the equation of motion for a simple harmonic oscillator and solve it step-by-step to get the general solution. Explain the physical meaning of each term in the solution.
	\item Starting from the expression $x(t)=x_0\cos{(\omega_0 t-\delta)}$, show the form of the trajectories followed by a simple harmonic oscillator in phase space (the phase diagram). Describe all relevant features of the phase diagram in terms of physical parameters of the system, such as total energy, amplitude, frequency, and mass. How would the phase diagram change if we introduce damping into the system?
	\item Write down the equation of motion for a damped harmonic oscillator under the influence of a damping force proportional to velocity. Solve the equation step-by-step and obtain the general solution in exponential form. Explain briefly how different types of damping based on the relationship between the damping coefficient and the natural frequency of the system lead to different forms of the motion (underdamping, overdamping, and critical damping).
	\item Write down and solve step-by-step the equation of motion for a driven damped harmonic oscillator under the influence of an external sinusoidal driving force to derive the expression for the amplitude and phase difference of steady-state oscillations. Explain the concept of resonance and how it manifests in this system.
	\item Define central force and describe its three main properties: No torque, motion in a plane, equal are in equal times. Show mathematically step-by-step how each property can be derived from the definition of central force.
	\item Describe how two-body problem can be reduced to an equivalent one-body problem. Derive the equation of motion for the equivalent one-body problem step-by-step.
	\item Write down the effective potential for inverse-square central force and draw its graph. Explain how different types of orbits (circular, elliptical, parabolic, hyperbolic) can be understood in terms of the effective potential and total energy of the system.
	\item Write down the effective force in a non-inertial reference frame including all non-inertial terms. Explain the physical meaning of each term in detail. Describe the cylonic motion in the atmosphere in terms of these non-inertial forces and explain why the wind blows counterclockwise (clockwise) around low-pressure areas in the northern (southern) hemisphere.

\end{enumerate}
