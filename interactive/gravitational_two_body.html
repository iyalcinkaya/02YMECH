Energy selects the conic: at fixed h, η = −1 gives a circle; −1 < η < 0 an ellipse; η = 0 a parabola; η > 0 a hyperbola. Energies below the circular minimum are inaccessible.

Model, units & classroom notes

Newtonian point masses, isolated from external forces. We set G = 1 and M = m₁ + m₂ = 1 in dimensionless units. The center of mass has zero position and velocity. The reduced particle is a mathematical representation of the separation, not a third physical body: O in the left panel is the force center in relative-coordinate space.

The energy slider sets η = 2Eh²/[μ(GM)²] = E/|Ecirc|, where Ecirc = −μ(GM)²/(2h²). Changing h holds η fixed, hence also eccentricity; it changes the orbit size and total energy. Changing the mass ratio holds M, h and η fixed: the relative orbit is unchanged, while μ, E, L and the individual paths change.

Both panels have identical spatial scales, fixed during each run. Orbit size is automatically fitted when parameters change; compare their scale bars. The left origin and right center of mass are marked by crosses. Body radii are symbolic. Optional velocity arrows use a common, fixed conversion within each run, separate from the position scale.

Motion is computed from the elliptic and hyperbolic Kepler equations, or Barker’s equation for a parabola. The clock advances uniformly in physical model time, so equal time intervals sweep equal areas. At 1×, one bound period or the displayed unbound encounter takes 24 seconds; the actual model-time rate is shown above. Bound orbits repeat. Unbound encounters start incoming at r = 8p, pass periapsis, and stop outgoing at r = 8p; the physical path continues to infinity.
