What the exponent means

Undamped · Pure oscillation
A purely imaginary exponent rotates z(t) at a constant radius. Its projection is a sinusoid with fixed amplitude.

Real part: Re λ = −γ controls the amplitude, |z(t)| = A e−γt. At γ = 0 the radius is constant; at γ > 0 it shrinks exponentially.

Imaginary part: Im λ = ω controls the rotation angle ωt + φ. The real-axis projection gives the observed displacement.

Drag λ to change both parameters at once. Drag the time slider to inspect any instant. For an underdamped mass–spring oscillator, ω here is the damped angular frequency and ω₀² = ω² + γ²; the parameters are selected independently in this demonstration.
Model: z(t) = A exp[(-γ + iω)t + iφ], t ≥ 0, γ ≥ 0. Positive ω rotates counterclockwise; negative ω rotates clockwise. Only non-growing modes (Re λ ≤ 0) are selectable. When ω = 0 the motion is exponential relaxation without oscillation.
